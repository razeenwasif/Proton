# Your task: frame the investigation

{{context}}

This is the first round. Write the research plan the team will follow:

- what exactly is being asked, and what would count as an answer;
- what is already known, with sources you have checked;
- the sub-questions it breaks into;
- how hypotheses will be tested — by derivation, by simulation or computation in the Lab, against published data.

Answer with:

- `plan`: the plan in Markdown, with TeX as `$...$` and `$$...$$`;
- `criteria`: what a good hypothesis must do, one per item;
- `questions`: the sub-questions, one per item;
- `queries`: two or three searches of the scholarly literature — arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex — that would find what is already known: a few plain keywords each, a phrase in quotes kept whole, such as `"rayleigh scattering" skylight spectrum`. The papers they find are added to the investigation and shown to everyone who proposes a hypothesis.
